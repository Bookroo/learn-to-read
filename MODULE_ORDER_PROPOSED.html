Generated from data/curriculum.json — edit that file and run scripts/build-module-order.py; do not edit this HTML.

The targetFluent on grade-1 text: ~60 words correct per minute at 95%+ accuracy.
Uppercase firstAll reading is intentionally uppercase through Lesson 44. Lowercase forms are consolidated in Lesson 45 before lowercase running text begins.
Teach reading from print to soundReaders meet the letters on the page first and decode them into sounds. Organize lessons around one spelling pattern and the sound or sounds it can represent—not around one sound followed by a list of all its possible spellings. Introduce alternative spellings in separate lessons so the reader learns one new visual pattern at a time.

Concepts & Exercises

Lesson rows show what is taught. This reference explains the named routines and strategies used to teach and practice it. Purple marks a concept or strategy; teal marks an exercise or recurring routine.

Concept or exerciseFirst introducedHow it works
Finger WordsLesson 1Give each sound a finger, touch and say the sounds, then slide across the knuckles to blend the word.
Elephant EarsLesson 1Say a word aloud. The reader gives a thumbs-up if it contains the target sound and a thumbs-down if it does not.
Teach TamLesson 1At the end of the lesson, the reader teaches Tam the new idea in their own words. Explaining it provides a quick understanding check.
Sticky SoundsLesson 3Identify sounds that connect smoothly to the sound after them and blend through the connection without adding an extra vowel.
Imaginary Finger WordsLesson 5Perform Finger Words mentally, without using a hand, as a bridge toward automatic decoding.
Buzz Check / BuzzerLesson 11Notice whether the voice box buzzes to distinguish voiced and unvoiced sounds, including the two sounds of s and th.
Flexible SoundsLesson 11Try the spelling's first common sound; if it does not form a real word, switch to the other taught sound.
Heart WordsLesson 14Map the sounds the reader already knows, identify the unexpected spelling, and learn only that part by heart.
Story WordsLesson 14Find a newly learned heart word inside real sentences, then read each complete sentence.
Brain WordsLesson 16Quickly reread previously decoded words so they become instantly recognizable and free attention for meaning.
Narrative NinjaLesson 17Reread a sentence carefully, hear an adult model it, then read it smoothly with expression.
Word ChainsLesson 18Read a sequence in which one letter changes at a time, such as ran → ram → ramp → camp.
Letter FriendsLesson 21Treat two letters that spell one sound as a single unit, such as sh or ch. Find the pair before blending the word.
Meet SyllablesLesson 31Listen to spoken words and clap or count their beats, then connect the -ing beat to the ends of written words.
Meet the VowelsLesson 33Identify the five vowel letters and discover that each can spell a short sound or its letter name.
Magic ELesson 34A silent final e changes the preceding vowel from its short sound to its long sound.
Lowercase Telling-ApartLesson 45Match capitals with lowercase partners, then spend extra time comparing shape-shifters and confusable forms such as b and d.
Two-Syllable SplittingLesson 50Begin with compounds, then prefixed words, then find the vowels, split between consonants, read each chunk, and join the chunks.
Open ChunksLesson 55When a syllable ends in a vowel, try the vowel's long sound before joining the chunks.
Lazy VowelLesson 55In an unstressed syllable, try the relaxed /uh/ sound when the carefully decoded word sounds almost right.
Fluency Checks I–IVLesson 57Use an unpracticed timed passage to measure accurate words per minute and chart growth. The final check uses two grade-1 passages and keeps the stronger score.
Vowel TeamsLesson 58Treat two neighboring vowel letters that spell one sound as a single unit.
Just-Read DaysLesson 68Add no new spelling. Read a longer connected text for accuracy, smoothness, stamina, and enjoyment.
Repeated ReadingLesson 68Read the same passage again, moving from accurate decoding toward smooth, expressive reading.
Bossy RLesson 80Notice that r changes the vowel sound before it, then connect ar, or, er, ir, ur, and later r-controlled spellings.
Reading with ExpressionLesson 100Use punctuation as a performance guide: questions rise, exclamations carry energy, and quotation marks signal dialogue.
Five-Finger RuleLesson 105Read one page of a possible independent book and raise one finger for each stuck word. Five fingers means save the book for later.
Cumulative ReviewLesson 106Revisit the major patterns, heart words, books, and games from all ten modules before the final fluency check.
GraduationLesson 107The reader completes a final book independently, celebrates the milestone, and receives guidance for continued daily reading.

Rule Before the Contractions Lesson

Before Lesson 77, make sure every contraction's full phrase has already been practiced in earlier lesson sentences or books. The reader should be able to read and understand the uncontracted phrase before learning the shortened form with an apostrophe.

Practice cannot, it is, I am, I am not, do not, that is, did not, I will, you are, let us, he is, there is, was not, you will, could not, I have, what is, they are, we are, will not, does not, she is, we will, is not, and I would or I had before introducing their contracted forms.
