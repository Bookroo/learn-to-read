Proposed Lesson Order — Modules 1–10

The targetFluent on grade-1 text: ~60 words correct per minute at 95%+ accuracy.
Uppercase firstAll reading is intentionally uppercase through Lesson 42. Lowercase forms are consolidated in Lesson 43 before lowercase running text begins.
Teach reading from print to soundReaders meet the letters on the page first and decode them into sounds. Organize lessons around one spelling pattern and the sound or sounds it can represent—not around one sound followed by a list of all its possible spellings. Introduce alternative spellings in separate lessons so the reader learns one new visual pattern at a time.

Concepts & Exercises

Lesson rows show what is taught. This reference explains the named routines and strategies used to teach and practice it. Purple marks a concept or strategy; teal marks an exercise or recurring routine.

Concept or exercise First introduced How it works
Finger Words Lesson 1 Give each sound a finger, touch and say the sounds, then slide across the knuckles to blend the word.
Elephant Ears Lesson 1 Say a word aloud. The reader gives a thumbs-up if it contains the target sound and a thumbs-down if it does not.
Teach Tam Lesson 1 At the end of the lesson, the reader teaches Tam the new idea in their own words. Explaining it provides a quick understanding check.
Sticky Sounds Lesson 3 Identify sounds that connect smoothly to the sound after them and blend through the connection without adding an extra vowel.
Imaginary Finger Words Lesson 5 Perform Finger Words mentally, without using a hand, as a bridge toward automatic decoding.
Buzz Check / Buzzer Lesson 11 Notice whether the voice box buzzes to distinguish voiced and unvoiced sounds, including the two sounds of s and th.
Heart Words Lesson 14 Map the sounds the reader already knows, identify the unexpected spelling, and learn only that part by heart.
Story Words Lesson 14 Find a newly learned heart word inside real sentences, then read each complete sentence.
Brain Words Lesson 16 Quickly reread previously decoded words so they become instantly recognizable and free attention for meaning.
Narrative Ninja Lesson 17 Reread a sentence carefully, hear an adult model it, then read it smoothly with expression.
Word Chains Lesson 18 Read a sequence in which one letter changes at a time, such as ran → ram → ramp → camp.
Letter Friends Lesson 21 Treat two letters that spell one sound as a single unit, such as sh or ch. Find the pair before blending the word.
Meet Syllables Lesson 25 Listen to spoken words and clap or count their beats. This is oral practice only until syllable reading begins.
Meet the Vowels Lesson 31 Identify the five vowel letters and discover that each can spell a short sound or its letter name.
Magic E Lesson 32 A silent final e changes the preceding vowel from its short sound to its long sound.
Lowercase Telling-Apart Lesson 43 Match capitals with lowercase partners, then spend extra time comparing shape-shifters and confusable forms such as b and d.
Two-Syllable Splitting Lesson 46 Begin with compounds, then find the vowels, split between consonants, read each chunk, and join the chunks.
Open Chunks Lesson 52 When a syllable ends in a vowel, try the vowel's long sound before joining the chunks.
Lazy Vowel Lesson 52 In an unstressed syllable, try the relaxed /uh/ sound when the carefully decoded word sounds almost right.
Flexible Sounds Lesson 11 Try the spelling's first common sound; if it does not form a real word, switch to the other taught sound.
Vowel Teams Lesson 55 Treat two neighboring vowel letters that spell one sound as a single unit.
Just-Read Days Lesson 65 Add no new spelling. Read a longer connected text for accuracy, smoothness, stamina, and enjoyment.
Repeated Reading Lesson 65 Read the same passage again, moving from accurate decoding toward smooth, expressive reading.
Fluency Checks I–III Lesson 75 Use an unpracticed timed passage to measure accurate words per minute and chart growth. The final check uses two grade-1 passages and keeps the stronger score.
Bossy R Lesson 76 Notice that r changes the vowel sound before it, then connect ar, or, er, ir, ur, and later r-controlled spellings.
Reading with Expression Lesson 96 Use punctuation as a performance guide: questions rise, exclamations carry energy, and quotation marks signal dialogue.
Five-Finger Rule Lesson 102 Read one page of a possible independent book and raise one finger for each stuck word. Five fingers means save the book for later.
Cumulative Review Lesson 103 Revisit the major patterns, heart words, books, and games from all ten modules before the final fluency check.
Graduation Lesson 104 The reader completes a final book independently, celebrates the milestone, and receives guidance for continued daily reading.

Rule Before the Contractions Lesson

Before Lesson 73, make sure every contraction's full phrase has already been practiced in earlier lesson sentences or books. The reader should be able to read and understand the uncontracted phrase before learning the shortened form with an apostrophe.

Practice cannot, it is, I am, I am not, do not, that is, did not, I will, you are, let us, he is, there is, was not, you will, could not, I have, what is, they are, we are, will not, does not, she is, we will, is not, and I would or I had before introducing their contracted forms.
